# Why we choose to blindly trust until something alert us, then we swing full way from trust to distrust
essay

Finding jobs these days aren't easy, and it's an excitement when people even invite you to the first part of the interview process. Whether or not you pass, at least that's an experience there, a confirmation that you're not good for nothing. Surely, in such elated moments, you'd just do whatever to get the job done, swear to yourself that, if you could get this job, you'd proved to yourself that you're worthy in this world. 

So there was a coding interview where one downloaded the files. React, node.js, these aren't something too difficult to understand; but the code are many, one can't be sure it'll be totally safe. But scanning through the stacks, _surely_ they are safe? Some code look old, like using old versions of the program, presumably haven't updated for long, so one could assume they have security issues, but we aren't writing for production, so that's fine. 

As one stressed through, trying to recall something one hadn't did in a year or two (in the age of internet, things update so quickly. What used to work no longer work in just half a year. When one started AI, it was kinda basic; and just a year after one quit AI in 2021, it became a fad, and everyone started launching their AI products), something kept blinking at the bottom right of one's eyes. In an effort to check what such notification are, after several times, it says that there are "Trojan:Script/Wacatac.H!ml" detected. Windows Defender automatically removed these, but they kept popping up, and one wonders why. So one went in and check. 

As some mentioned, this script is usually false positives, but one could not be sure. One did not dare take the risk, so after Windows once more deleted, one deleted all the files one'd cloned from GitHub, and moved them to sandbox. God wonders, one had got one's previous computer infected by virus before, therefore after one changed computer, it was a pain in the arse setting up all over again. But since then, one had been using sandboxes to download and scanned for stuffs before one move them to one's computer. Those websites one trusted more one don't use sandboxes, but neither do one directly open it immediately. Why, in any case, will the hirer be more trustible than these downloads? Do they not also have malicious intents as a possibility? 

Perhaps our will to earn money blinds us from these possibilities. We easily fall into the trap when we thought we're just there for the money, and ended up we became the money for _them_. What says those news that mentioned of people accepting jobs online and got sold to what countries to do slavery works? Those countries, their government holding them weak, had their own plots of land held by companies, and companies acting as government, not dissimilar to British East India Company and the equivalent in Europe in the past making parts of Southeast Asia, America, and other parts of the world their colonies; only latter do the government interfere and ask them to disseminate their military forces, and hand over their colonies to the government. While seeking work may not be that extreme, they might try to steal your personal information, and that, one now believes, but not before. 

And when one was alert with that possible trojan, one stopped all work at once, trying to seek down the company's information. Ask, why do one only seek so late; should not we seek when we're searching for jobs? Most people who search for jobs knows, they send application to thousands of companies, and if one have to search through one by one, it'd be a pain in the arse taking 30 years to finish. OK, that's an exaggeration, but you get the idea. Say, let's limit to those we receives application and searches for it. One already speak about oneself, in terms of elation, for one receives little acceptance. But what of the others? One doubt. First, they may not know to search for the company beforehand until passing all the interviews and everything so so. Second, not everyone is tech people, and one wonders how many people don't have malware and some tracking on their computer that read what they're doing. Third, at the preliminary stage, some people are so successful they still have a lot of acceptance, and crunching through all interviews is already hard, what says deeply investigating the company? 

But in the end, one do investigate the company, for the sake of safety. So one started searching for the company's name that matches the logo; OK, established in 2024 and had no posts or whatever on LinkedIn page of theirs, and only one member, but it listed they have 11-50 members. Presumably, not everyone have LinkedIn, and that's OK. Then, one asked AI to search for them, and they found the company used to file in with California ages ago, and established 1981, had been "Suspended Ftb". Now that's weird, as that doesn't match the country listed on LinkedIn, nor does the starting date. Oh, how wondrous are us human animals at trying to convince ourselves without letting others convince us -- one already convince oneself, in their defense, they might be coming back to life again after long. And one also knows of foreign people who can register a company in the US, so that 'explains'. But one don't know, and neither is one sure, but one's gotta play safe. Everything seemed out of place, but because one took action oneself, it had been more easier to try convince oneself than to unconvince oneself, a drawback of taking stance. 

This process had not been easy, because trying to keep sensitive in mind is draining lots of energy. While other activities may kept one energetic for long till the afternoon before feeling exhausted, and some repetitive jobs (like reading Chinese Novels) repeatedly drain one's energy as time passes by, it had not been as draining as staying alert. Just an hour later, one already felt oneself weak to the point of losing appetite, not feeling like doing those activities that one used to enjoy, mind constantly worry about whether one's computer's infected or not in one's brain, and if one want to check, all the hassle one had to go through (and one hate hassle through these -- people sure do know how to take advantage of our hatred towards doing more than less) installing antivirus software (multiple of them, using one, delete/disable it, then use another, just so they overlap each other's weaknesses -- don't think that one antivirus can cover all viruses, but multiple with different dictionaries can cover most, and only most, not all). Urgh, and not to say boot up in safe mood and all the time involved. My fellow humans, 10 years is 3650 days, and if you spend quite a few days on it, just think how much shorter do you have in your life? Think in terms of week, it's 520 (approximately), and if you spend one week on that, or if you get infected 520 times and spend 1 week on each to solve the problem, how many weeks have you wasted? Life is short. 

One can't be sure one can or cannot trust. In the end, they might be innocent themselves, using libraries that're infected, perhaps? Nor if one stop running the program does it notify one. Theodore Zeldin had a will to know everyone on Earth, all strangers.
