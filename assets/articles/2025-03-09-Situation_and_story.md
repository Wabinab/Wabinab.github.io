# The Situation and the Story by Vivian Gornick
afterthought writing

The whole book basically speaks about having a persona/narrator in your story -- and you need to get that correct, who is it that's speaking, and who's the person you're speaking of, from who's person point of view -- to make a good, attention-sucking story. And then author listed various examples and various ways one can do that. Because the book is short itself, one'll not care to summarize it here. 

As one's reading about the book, one'd been thinking about one's own writing -- what's essentially missing, the persona. One tried to make one's own example, albeit short ones, and didn't even go through the process of editing (because one hate editing), and didn't even take too long to write. Nevertheless, one first thought about the subject, whom one wanted to write about, put oneself in their shoes, and gave a flawed impression of the story (because putting oneself in their shoes means one uses one's feeling-tentacles to see how one would feel and act in their shoes, but not how _they_ would feel and act in their own shoes). This gave rise to ['I' want to write about her](https://wabinab.github.io/article?filename=2025-03-05-About_Her.md) that focuses on a friend of mine that one still consider friend but she maybe not because she no longer wants to reply to one, for whatever reason; and one later write about [Wrestling Against Time](https://wabinab.github.io/article?filename=2025-03-08-time.md) as another story about oneself, a memoir about a specific part of me fighting. Albeit, the story isn't perfect, as one said, due to no editing, and it'd been quite rushed, esp. the 2nd part. So both might not be that good, but at least they're steps to write better stories. Or, in the rarest of case, if one feels like it, which one usually don't, one may rewrite this story eons later in better format. 

And of course, one also wants to integrate it into one's translated books. Previously before one reached the conclusion of the book, one thought one could only write about oneself and the stories that one'd gone through oneself; but when author showed one a story written by her student about her student's grandma and grandad, one knows, her student had put herself in her grandma's shoes, and hence can write a story about the _emotions_ of her grandma as seen from her narrating perspective. Therefore, who says Xenoblade Chronicles one can't do that also? So, for [History & The Promised World](https://wabinab.github.io/xenoblade-future-redeemed/11_histories.html), one'll try to make that inclusive.
