# Joys and Sorrows: Pablo Casals as told to Albert E. Kahn
afterthought biography 

Oh, how one wish one had a mum like his, supportive of his work and see it right through him. But no, most people aren't capable of doing that, and not one's mum. Indeed, there was a mixture of supportive and unsupportive one felt during one's course; and because humans are complex and complicated, she could support me on one issue and discourage me on that same issue! All in all, whenever one wanted to do something, it's a boat going against the stormy current. Oh, how one hoped one had a for-current like Pau (Pablo) Casals. 

"_What you do with your talent is what matters._" 80% of a specific talent is available only to 20% of the people. That's 80/20 rule. Because there are a myriad of talents, each of us would have at least one of those myriad, each of us would at least be the 20% in some aspects. And of course, in other aspects, we're in the 80%. There are only so much time we enjoy, and they're insufficient to nurture all our talents, so most of our 20% will stay 20%, while those we choose to nurture may go to top 1%. In technical (mathematical) terms, 80% of specific talent goes to 20% of people, but within that 20% of people, 99% of talent goes to 1% of people. That's just heuristics, so let's not do the math that prove 'common nonsense'. 

"_In the language of everybody!_" When one write, one loves to use language that expresses what one felt. Specific words won't have the definition that most people use, but one would provide the definition for what one think about the word. But alas, it means that not everyone could understand what one think, not even after reading the definition one provided, what says not everyone read the definition one provides. (Remember the 80/20 above -- only 20% of the people have the ability to understand that a word can have multiple definitions. The rest of the 80% cannot fathom why words can't just have a single definition but can have many.) It also means, when you read my books, you'll see words that aren't defined like the common meaning. From _How to Read a Book_ by Mortimer Adler, that's great -- you'll have to find out what the author means by the specific word. Though in music, how could you even define them? One don't know -- perhaps that's why Pau's teacher asked him to play "in the language of everybody". 

"_To learn from the past, but not be shackled by it._" Great advice, but difficult to follow. From _Lessons of the past: the use and misuse of history_ by Ernest May, we know that people tend to stick to histories they'd experienced in their living lifetime, rather than taking history from before they were born. That's a kind of shackle they put on themselves, believing that the future would look _more likely_ with the recent past than with the distant past. What folly! After all, if everyone knows what had happened in the recent past, they would do their best, _collectively_, to prevent it from happening; but they would forget about the lessons from more distant past they seldom/never experienced, and repeated the folly. Perhaps we are just shackles of history, their puppets. 

_Integrity, simplicity_. Easily spoken, hard to do. How many of us didn't have integrity? A lot, including one. The moment you speak a lie, black or white, for whatever reason, to whoever (including yourself), you're breaking an integrity. Or perhaps, you said you want to do something, but you failed to do it (which one always encountered -- one said one want to go to bed by 11p.m., but the Chinese novel got too addictive, so it became 11.30 p.m., one knows the time, but one still continued reading, because it's addictive. An integrity lost). Simplicity is more difficult to speak for. Not all of the things we have in life demands simplicity -- sometimes, if we simplify, it loses its complexity, and that's a bad bad bad. Instead, in such situation, we should live with the complexity and not try to simplify it, like in _Overcomplicated_ by Samuel Arbesman, or _Complications: A Surgeon's Notes on an Imperfect Science_ by Atul Gawande. Not everything is mathematics, you know. The "assume the cow is spherical" or "assume Earth is spherical" doesn't work. Cow aren't spherical (you know how the cow shaped like -- complex), neither is Earth (with all its mountains and seas and crevasses, it's irregular). Even in physics -- **we assume energy is conserved, but in fact, it's not.** That's because energy is conserved only in an empty universe, and our universe, filled with stars and galaxies and milky ways and planets, are far from empty -- so energy is NOT conserved in our universe. 

"_It's not only what you say that's important, but also how you say it._" "_Success didn't come overnight._" One don't need to elaborate on these -- there're already a lot of writings on them you can search online. 

"_People can sometimes be made to believe very foolish things via agenda/propaganda._" According to _A Hopeful History_ by Rutger Bregman, that's very true, as long as you convinced people that they're doing it for the greater good. The people of Nazis, influenced by Adolf Hitler, didn't think they're destroying the world like the rest of the world did. They _sincerely_ believe that what they're doing will make the world a better place. And it's not their fault, unfortunately, regardless of how you feel right now, reading this sentence -- you should feel compassionate for them, not hatred nor anger -- one daresay, with 99% accuracy, if you're the one in his/her shoes, you'd likely believed the same as he/she, regardless of how you felt right now (denial? 'Impossible!'?). 

_Principles._ One had read Ray Dalio's principles for quite long already, but one hadn't put it into practice. Perhaps it's about time to do that -- to use what one read than to let it rot. And it seemed like quite a lot of people obey to their principles, putting their principles into practice. But of course, "quite a lot" is by numbers, not by percentage. By percentage, they're probably a mere 0.0001% of people, but by numbers 0.0001% of 8 billion people is a huge number -- indeed, 8000 people.
