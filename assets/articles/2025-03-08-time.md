# Wrestling Against Time
memoir

The Internet imprisons time. All the beautiful lights and the addictive Chinese novels, they captured one's attention from leaving it. When noon time arrives, one'd say, "Just one more chapter, please," and one after one after one, before one realizes it, it's one p.m. Forced with either facing the terror shouts of one's mum or one surrender oneself before she calls out, one had no choice but to chop off the roots that attached one's eyes to the screen, the contents to my heart. Losing a part of one's heart is heartbreaking, but her shouts are more impending. 

Tiny cycles aggregate into large cycles. At night, one's supposed to start exercising at six, take about 45 mins to complete, and have dinner at seven. But oh, how roots grow so fast. The few tentacles had extended its reach, grew its moustaches, breached the surface of one's heart yet again, and glued one's eyes on the addictive content of Chinese novels. As one's eyes flopped between the clock on the lower right corner of one's screen and back to the content, one knew one had to read faster. Faster and faster one read, until one skip chapters. At least, based on the title of such chapter, one can guess its interestingness. Speed reading is never fun, for when you pick up pace, your brain naturally tries to adapt; and in speed, headache is frequent. Your eyes started flopping here and there, which, if you're not used to seeing video games faster than 24 FPS, the food that you just ate or the air in your stomach will push itself up to the upper limit of your throat; you feel you might throw any time soon. It's the same feeling even without watching video games at 120 FPS. When you got used to skipping novels, you no longer can slow down and read it, prefer to spend the next hour just pressing 'next' and skipped through the entirety. What's worse than reading 300 news for the next hour just by reading its headlines! 

So, time passed and passed and passed until one cannot bear it anymore, and it's 8 o'clock. Time to start the exercising. With some lack of efficiency, one reluctantly stick one's butt out but eyes remained glued to the screen for another 10 minutes before really getting up. Between exercises, sometimes one had to come back and read one chapter before continuing. And by the time one had dinner, it's 9 o'clock. Southeast Asian had the disease called sleep paralysis, where the exact cause is unknown, but some says, if you eat too full 2 hours before sleeping, you might die in your sleep as one possible reason. And since one eat that late, one cannot sleep that early. By the time one finished, it's 9:30 p.m, and that leaves the earliest time to get to bed 11:30 p.m. 

Time fighter being time fighter. One said one will sleep early at 11:30 so one can get up early in the morning the next day at 7 to see the morning sun still dimmed in the horizon, when the temperature is nice and cool, shady and refreshing. This time, one gets clever. You don't read novels just before bed, just in case one get glued to the screen again. So, one decided to read some non-fiction that's not as interesting as such addictive Chinese novels. True, some non-fiction are quite addictive, and one may finish them as late as 0h, but it's better than the never-ending works of fiction. Just as one put down one's keyboard, closed the PDF reader, closed Obsidian/Marktext, a voice rung in one, told me, "Just one more chapter." Such voice loomed large especially when you're sleepy, especially when all your intents to resist had already been exhausted earlier in the day, and so one succumb to such lure, one more chapter. 

One more become one more become one more, and here we are again, 0:40 it says. My body temperature, from what one told one's circadian clock one's sleeping time, had already been dropping steadily for the past hour; the air that was previously hot now felt cold. The outside air had lost its heat to space without extra heat to warm it back up. My heart and stomach and liver and internal organs burns as my body tried to cleanse the poison they'd accumulated during waking hours. My blood tried to penetrate the blood-brain barrier, to communicate with the liquid that washes one's brain and bring out the toxins slowly by slowly during sleeping time. One can feel the taste of iron behind one's esophagus, in the tip of the spinal cord connecting to one's brain, and the back of one's brain. One can feel the fire that engulfed one's torso as one's body bent forward more and more as the flames started to feel pain. Until one had to give up all as concentration no longer works, and the unpleasant feeling overcame the pleasant dopamine in numbers, closed the light, drink a cup of water, go to toilet, and went to sleep. 

On the bed, one tossed and rolled as one missed the 'correct' time of sleeping. When your internal cleaning system is already at work and you're not yet asleep, it's harder to fall asleep. Plus, the sense of guilty, of calling yourself the warrior against time's pull, felt stronger when you failed the battle yet again, for yet another day and night. In the long term, one felt exhausted, decide not to care anymore for what time one eats lunch and dinner, but still for the time one sleeps, for one had to wake up the next day, despite one hour late. And the cycle repeats again. 

Bullshit. 

Just before lunch, one told oneself one had to finish this first. Feeling guilty, one sometimes swapped to read some non-fiction to dampen the guiltiness, though the time to eat lunch usually don't change unless one cut before the idea of reading novels developed. 

Just before dinner, one already watched the clock, half past six, that one should be exercising. But one felt, it wasn't that pressure yet, one could do it at 7 and still eat at 8. And at 7, one looked at the clock and felt no strong pressure yet, and said one could do it at 7:30 and eat at 8:30. At 7:30, one looked at the clock and felt a pressure, and it's about time to stop, so one prepared to finish whatever one's at hand. And by the time one finished, it's around 7:55, and one slowly and guiltily doved around to get everything prepared for exercising, and that reached about 8:10. One already knew, not once, not twice, but more than thrice, that the time already tell it, it's time to do exercise, but one had not take actions to unglued one's eyes from the screen and one's butt from one's chair, instead, let it pass, and suffer from guiltiness later. 

At dinner, one told oneself, tomorrow, tomorrow one'll fixed such schedule. Sure, one does fixed it, but **regression** means that, day after day, one starts with eating dinner at 8, and the next day, at 8:15, then 8:30, then 8:45, until it's back at 9. The cycle therefore don't repeat itself again as one had to muster the willpower to do it once again, only to get regressed back to its 'original' timeframe. No matter what one does, the regression stays. 

And because dinner happens that way, sleep happens the same way. Remember, 2 hours after dinner only can one sleep, to prevent sleep-death. Plus, it's not the only cause that causes one's sleep to regress to the late. Guiltiness no longer helps as strongly as one wants it to. Nor does the signal one's body tells one -- only on the verge of collapse do one had no choice but to obey. 

Oh, how one wish there's no Chinese novels invented to pull one's attention. Oh, how one wishes one can discipline oneself. "Admit!" Even if there's no Chinese novels, there'll be some non-fiction that gazed one's eyes until one looked once more at the watch and it says, 0:30, time to sleep.
