# Journey into the Rainforest
memoir

Hiking alone and hiking with someone else is a different experience. When hiking with someone else, the focus is on that someone else, to improve your relationships; the trek only serves as a path, an opportunity, that makes such deepening possible. Walking alone, however, your focus is on the nature. 

Where we parked our cars, going uphill, the sounds of speeding cars in the distance can still be heard in the background. Thin foliage does not shield such booming noise from being heard. The sun that heats the ground was up ahead, and the heat was not too bearable. Humidity was high, and sweat pores tend not to release moisture. 

Vegetations were low mostly, consisting of thick shrubs barely reaching one's waist. Occasionally, tall tree barks that looked like they're useful for the furniture could be seen, it's texture nice to see and nice to touch; but these were rare. 

![Useful bark](assets/images/useful_bark.jpg)

![Low Vegetations](assets/images/low_vegetations.jpg)

Occasionally, one might hear some animals, perhaps monkeys, jumping from tree to tree, but they kept quiet. Only the rustling of leaves sounded as they moved quietly behind the scene. On the ground, insects were not rare, but they were usually wasps that make holes in the ground and built their nest inside the soil, between roots. 

As one moved deeper into the forest, everything dimmed down. Foliage of low were replaced by those of tall. But hey, no worry; despite no one trekking the trail, you only had to worry about the insects and monkeys and not tripping yourself off the relatively-wide ledge from a flicker of attention, getting too lost in the beauty. Plus, trekking the top of hills, the sun shines bright; you could easily make your way back if you want to. The signs are one reassurance, your memory are another, but most of all, being at the top, you believe you can easily be seen by people. No matter how far in, you just had to follow the trails. They'll lead you to your destinations. 

Yet, if you want to see a small waterfall at the bottom, there's no other way but to descent. The descent was steep, the use of a walking stick is recommended to keep your feet stick. Tree barks weren't always a help, for some were infested by ants that bites. The ground you had to watch, for you don't want to stand on the rights of way of ants that bites. Plus, you neither want to trip going down the pit. There was a place (no image taken) where butterflies had infested the ground in dozens. They easily camouflaged to the leaves; even one cannot see them until they flies from their standing location. Do they make that their home, or do they just went for the fruit dropped from above? The noise of the birds in the dozens started chirping bright and clear, the only noise in the forest that stood out as they argued within their family. Perhaps the cicada might have made noise, but one got so used to it that one didn't distinguish. 

One's heart started to falter. The roads weren't quite clear: though you'd supposed the foliage to border the path, creating a clear rights of way, it isn't so. Some places, there were no undergrowth, only trees, and leaves everywhere makes it hard to distinguish between the rights of way and just a basin of leaves. One's attention had to focus more on not getting lost, while also taking care of all the other dangers aforementioned. Is this a way going left, or is it the one on the right up ahead? Sweat perspirated at a higher rate, as the idea that few people come and go -- in fact, one hadn't seen anyone for more than a quarter of an hour -- might mean one get lost and GPS might not work to locate one. No, there was a problem trying to open up the map on my phone, one remembers! It seems to not support it. Do one want to continue descent, or do one want to turn back? 

Continue therefore, but one had picked up one's steps. One just wished to reach the place of interest -- the mini waterfall -- as quickly as possible. Minutes past and there was no sign of getting to the bottom. Did one walked the wrong road? Is it not supposed to be straight? One'd been going right then left then right for quite a long while, is this even the right way? There's only one road though... Or maybe, the basin of leaves had concealed another? One can't distinguish. One can't see the sun; the thick foliage had blocked most of it -- only the reflection of sunlight through pores between leaves covering the tops. All the while one cut through spider webs that sticks onto the surface of one's skin: did one get bitten by any? Do you feel anything if you get bitten by a spider? You don't feel paper cuts until much later, though, washing under water. In contrast, ants inject acid to the wounds, causing you to immediately feel the pain. 

Then one told oneself, if one wants to enjoy oneself, one had to go through it. If, in the future, one wants to camp outside deep in the rainforest, one had to purge the unknown, the fear of getting oneself lost. Of course, getting someone else by one's side greatly encourages one's courage, but now that no one is by one's side, one had to take care to not get oneself in danger, for one may never exit if that is. Deeper and deeper we go, until, aha, one heard water flow. That might be it. Quickly, let's take a look and get out of here. 

![Waterfall](assets/images/waterfall.jpeg)

The way back up was more easier. Retreating via the known had always been easier than pursuing the unknown. Even when one's attention faltered a bit, losing memory of a section of the road one walked before realizing one'd faltered, one'd be sure one'll reach back up. Though, one do have to gather more attention just in case one accidentally walked into the basin than the actual road, for one was using intuition; memory might not be too trustworthy. The feeling of uncertainty lingers until one reached the top of the hill again; and since the foliage changed to one clear path, it's easier to recognize. One just had to remember which side one'd walked and not went the wrong turn! 

Upon exiting, it's a wonder what insects take refuge at the different naturgemalde. Layers weren't just going up a hill, but also in concentric circles going deeper into the forest. it was exhausting but heart-palpering. Perhaps the next time, we'll try another route again. For now, let's return to one's car and get on the journey home.
