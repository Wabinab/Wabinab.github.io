# Metamorphosis by Franz Kafka
afterthought great_books

On the surface, it's a novel about a guy living in a family of 4: his dad and mum, and his younger sister, and himself; he, as the sole breadwinner of the family whom seldom come home and know what they do when he was out at work day and night, all week long, suddenly found himself transformed into a 'beast'. He don't really know what he'd transformed into, for there were no mirrors in his room. However, he felt weird, trying to get out of bed is difficult, given the new situation he's in. To the point where his boss come to find out what happened, he tried explaining, but to no avail. Opening the door as he got used to it, they found him an animal. OK, that's the first part. Then, the second part, his 3 other family members went out to work and take care for him. He sometimes get to eavesdrop on their conversations, or anytime, find himself fun things, like crawling up the ceilings. But going into part 3, when he tried to get out of the room, he got damaged by his dad. Afterward, he got so weak he couldn't really do anything. When time goes on and no improvement seen, his own sister suggests dumping him, herself in a nervous breakdown state, and declare him not her brother but an animal, and that he couldn't understand them when he could. In one incident, he crawled out, and get back in, but too effete, he agreed with his sister. He had to go; and with one last look out the window, he closed his eyes forever, dried up, and became dried millipede, never even transformed back. The cleaner that came in everyday that weren't scared of him (the only one whom weren't scared of him, actually; his families were _forced_ to not feel scared of him, associating him with the lovely brother she had, the lovely child they had) dump him in a plastic bag and get rid of it. And the last day, shall their parents look forward to find a husband for their girl. And that's the end of the story. 

Till the end, his parents don't know he actually can understand their speaking, but they can't, for his voicebox changed to that of a millipede and it came out... weird. 

Like all children story, there's morals of the story. Not a single one, but a lot. Let's start from Gregor's perspective. His life was 'normal' until the transformation kicked him out of his comfort zone. Even then, the first thing, he wasn't even curious about his own state, trying to get back to work. 

> _They had so much to worry about at present that they had lost sight of any thought for the future._

Consider how many (or how much, in uncountable nouns) of us, when met with something such, would first tend in to our worries than whatever happen to us. How his parents and his boss did hurt one but not him -- for he already got used to the situation to meet _their_ expectations, living _their_ lives, forced or otherwise, and hope to get _respect_ in turn of his (slavery-like) service. When you start working to death, your boss want you to work to death, your parents and families treat you working to death as the 'expected', even if the demands is unreasonable; and any change would disturb their inner peace, or rather, pricking a needle into their inner comfort zone. And that don't feels good, what's unknown. 

Act 2: Now that his 'present' crisis had been solved, his boss had made known, his dad had drove him back into the room with a stick until he's heavily injured, he could now explore more about himself, such as, what could he do with the thousand little legs on his body? What about the patches on the shells? Or, as included in the story, how he could use his antennas? When there's nothing urgent, you tend to the important. Now that nothing is forcing you to divert your attention away, your worries fade, and you can focus on _curiosity_ a little more. 

> _For some reason, the tall, empty room where he was forced to remain made him feel uneasy as he lay there flat on the floor, even though he had been living in it for five years._

When the external faded, the internal came, and one started asking philosophical questions about oneself. His sister, whom were more brave, brought him food everyday, though they had to try out what he likes eating; and that's rotten food he founds yummy after several trials. (This we'll come back later). It soothed his mind, after he found out there were money left in his father's deposits after his dad's business failed. He never asked, quickly dived into saving the family like a hero of strength. What pushed him to his extreme were his family's state, and her sister's dream -- to go to the music conservatory requires a lot of money. His father especially didn't want to see him, so he hid himself. His mother was eager, but his dad and his sister hid him from her view, restrained her choice. (Well, the old centuries, women are the possession of their father, their husband, their son, never herself). Luckily for him, and for most of us, we quickly forget our sorrows and lived on -- life still had to be lived, right? So we naturally get used to stuffs, even if that stuff should not be get used to, we tell ourselves. But hey, it's something that he can't change, so he could only get use to it or die from Mother Nature's selection of the fittest. His mom, though, had hope that one day, he would transform back; but not him anymore. 

> _He had come very close to forgetting, and it had only been the voice of his mother, unheard for so long, that had shaken him out of it. Nothing should be removed; everything had to stay; he could not do without the good influence the furniture had on his condition; and if the furniture made it difficult for himt o crawl about mindlessly that was not a loss but a great advantage._

He tried to protect his belongings, but to no avail. He may not realized, but he was now under the care of his sister, and that means he had no free will but to obey. Communications was out of question -- most of us only like our dogs because they obey us, not because they, like humans, can 'betray' us. In the latter case, we get annoyed; but words reached a child cannot reach a dog, for the latter don't understand what we're speaking about, at least not totally, only the body language part in limited sense. 

Act 3: 

> _No one dared to remove the apple lodged in Gregor's flesh, so it remained there as a visible reminder of his injury. he had suffered it there for more than a month..._

Condition changed again, and another round of getting used to it begun. His sister, got used to his state, no longer take extra care, for she picked up her worries and all that to spare attention for him after coming home from an exhausting day's work. His desire for attention therefore wasn't quenched. One time, at the wish of those 3 guys whom rented a room in their house, offered his sister to come out and play violin. He thought that, now it's his time to made them understand that he could listen to them, by crawling out of the room and dance to the music. 

> _Was he an animal if music could captivate him so?_

But you know, when you try to convey something, it's not always that 'something' that gets understood. Indeed, his family, at the brink of despair, despaired. When the 3 guys asked his dad, his dad respectfullessly drove them back into their room, obsessed with them not seeing Gregor in his current state. His sister, instead of understanding him, lost all wishes, and asked their parents to dump him. At one time did his dad questioned whether he could understand their speech or not, but his sister, without a second thought, denied. He felt abandoned , but he wished the best for his family nonetheless. 

> _"It's got to go", shouted his sister, "that's the only way, Father. You've got to get rid of the idea that that's Gregor. We've only harmed ourselves by believing it for so long. How can that be Gregor?..."_

His sister's right, for him. He turned around and return back to his room, where his sister bang and locked it from the other side. 

> _"What now, then?", Gregor asked himself as he looked round in the darkness. He soon made the discovery that he could no longer move at all... If it was possible, he felt that he must go away even more strongly than his sister. He remained in this state of empty and peaceful rumination until he heard the clock tower strike three in the morning. He watched as it slowly began to get light everywhere outside the window too. Then, without his willing it, his head sank down completely, and his last breath flowed weakly from his nostrils._

And that's the end of him, dead and removed. 

Now, let's move on to the perspective of their family. Consider, therefore, that one day, you're met with something new, whatever it is. Something met you by surprise: a war raged at the outskirt of your town/city, or perhaps, you lose your job, or COVID strike. Seeing missiles flew over the air, people may perhaps started talking to each other, or more normally behaved, they behave as if nothing happened. Their brains were hijacked, they hadn't been trained to react to the event. Therefore, when nothing serious happens, what one can do is to not react to it, as if it never happened. Disbelievement is the key. Then, gradually, you come to accept such event as fate; our natural skills provided by Mother Nature let us _get used_ to whatever it is happening. Forget about the curses and complaints; there's nothing else we can do about it other than to live with it. In terms of COVID, that means, at the beginning, "why can't we just imprison those getting the virus and the rest of us just live like normal?" attitude. And later, when everyone on lockdown, either rebel, trying to stay 'normal' times, or despair, hoping the return to 'normal'. Yet, things have complete changed; we can no longer return to the past state. As years passed by and people can walked back the ground, they'd become so used to the virus it was just called another common cold. 

But there's another part of the story. While some situation we can tolerate living with it, other situations only get worse the more we interact with it; and we never get used to it, for it is forever changing. Say, assume that COVID don't stay as they are but mutate faster than our vaccine can protect us, so that even after vaccination, the mutated type on a vaccinated person caused as strong and deadly a sickness as the original type on an unvaccinated person, and discovery of vaccine always lacked mutation speed. Such is when we say, "it has to die." One don't know if it's possible, but that's what the people centuries ago did with smallpox -- before it mutate, they eradicate it completely. Or perhaps, its mutation speed just isn't fast enough to catch up with vaccination invention speed, one don't know. And only when it die can you return to a 'normal' life. Or rather, something had changed -- just like Gregor had left the family forever -- but a new equilibrium had been made, their minds unshackled from the past, and time to look forward into the future.
