# Experimenting With Lots of AI and Found These
ai models choices

There are a lot of AI models out there, but one found out, when using them for solving programming/coding problems, the difference between how they reply. Their difference can range from frustrations to straight to the point. 

Weaker models tend to be faster because they're smaller, and if they don't think for long, they usually reply based on their memory rather than searching up for the newest details. This means that you may not get the reply you want, or they may confidently give you wrong detail. For example, if you ask them about something that happens after their training cutoff, they may say, "I cannot find... in my memory. I think you mean ..." **This happens even if they can search the internet.** It's inherent in the training process that, even when given the tools to search, they don't necessarily use it if they can retrieve it "from their memory." 

For some houses of models (usually of the same company), they remain stupid even after you try to correct them; particularly, they're _overconfident_ on their replies. You don't say, this happens to MOST houses of models, perhaps because they had similar training process on similar data despite their structural differences. 

It's also why, when you ask the same question across multiple AI (one tried it on Z.ai, DeepSeek, and StepFun), they all replied similarly, with very little differences. It's not necessarily true for EVERY query, but for some query that one particularly need cross verification, one cannot get what one want. 

That's when an AI that _particularly_ can get what one want stood out: **Claude Sonnet 4.6.** One don't know why, but it had what other AI models lacked -- the ability to verify itself whether it's hallucinating or not. One don't have a subscription, so one don't know about Opus, but Haiku does have that ability too, although it's less capable of it. 

Say, for example, you cross check the results given by an AI and told it that this doesn't exist. Sonnet will check the internet and try to verify whether it is hallucinating or not. On the other hand, if you're interacting with DeepSeek, for example, from the "thinking" block, you can see it search the internet far crazier and far faster than any other AI does, but it seems like those context it acquired had made it more hallucinated than before, by leading me down the wrong path more and more, until one had to delete the whole code that it generated and recreate it myself, without the help of AI (esp. when one got rate limited on Claude). 

That means one had to change how one's interacting with different AI. 

With Sonnet, one can communicate with it like one's **brainstorming** with another person. It can provide ideas, cross-verify mine, etc. It is, of course, not always correct, and there _are_ times when it cannot get what one said, especially when one don't say it clearly or when one had a temper and purposefully don't want to say it clearly. But overall, if one interact with it clearly, it'll usually get what one want, with some humor. 

On the other hand, see DeepSeek as a **tool** to achieve what you want. **One must do all the thinking myself, IGNORE all the suggestions that DeepSeek give, and focus like a laser beam on how one's going to make my code work.** Repeat: whatever suggestions DeepSeek gives, IGNORE! Treat it as a tool to accomplish your goal. Don't divert from where you can see the endpoint, because, believe me, DeepSeek cannot see where you want to go, unlike Claude Sonnet which can somewhat derive where you want to go, although not the full picture, just like any other humans cannot read your mind directly but had to guess from clues, or ask you to tell them bluntly about it. DeepSeek lacked the ability to do that, so it can only be a follower that trails behind your butt but never lead the way in front. You must lead it where you want to go, and you had to see it much more clearly than if you'd interact with Claude because DeepSeek won't help you see your goal in mind -- you had to visualize it first before you interact with it. 

And because one interact with DeepSeek the 2nd most after Claude, that's why one took it as an example. Z.ai, on the other hand, took too long to reply, don't bother. But when it did, it also needs straightforward guidance (one's speaking about GLM 5.1). 

StepFun 3.5, on the other hand, have higher level of hallucinations compared to DeepSeek and Z.ai, creating inexistent objects as if they exist. It may even tell you that it cannot search the internet, even though it's clear that you give it the tools in the toolbar. But two good things about StepFun: first, the tok/sec output is very fast; not the fastest, but much much faster than, say, DeepSeek and most other AI. On the same hand, it **also sometimes, but not always, had a different fresh viewpoint that actually becomes a viable solution.** While DeepSeek and Z.ai may have lower "temperature" (they less seldom think outside the box), Step seems to sometimes, out of luck or what, comes up with the correct solution to solve the error that other AI, not even Claude, comes up with. That's what fun about StepFun. 

Lumo was my go-to in the past, but it had degraded significantly in terms of frontend interface. The first message is OK, but after a few messages, their encrypt-decrypt kinda clogged up the frontend so while your browser starts to lag while it churns out the data on the backend. Particularly, since one started to experiment on [Modal](https://modal.com/pricing) on how models work, one realized "streaming" means returning one single word at a time, with all the unnecessary json, which is a waste of bandwidth. One also want to do my own encryption, and if that means encrypting every single word and decrypting every single word, just imagine how much CPU is spent decoding those and put it back. It certainly will slow you down. So if one's going to deal with encryption, one couldn't "stream" the data back one word at a time, but one needs to "buffer" and send back a chunk at a time, where a chunk could mean, say, 100 words, and so to the user, the display would be different from how they expect streaming to be, but chunks after chunks appear to stamp. 

But that's OK, since Time To Last Token (TTLT) is what matters anyways, not seeing the stream (unless if you have OCD). 

The good thing one loves about Lumo and Duck.ai are their privacy AND **no need for login.** Sometimes, one may be renting an instance or on a computer outside, and one don't want to login to use AI, which all these other providers keep bugging me with their login interface that had no way to close (not even with "Click to Remove Element" extension) and fed me up. 

How about hosting my own models on my computer? It can work, but one had shitty hardwares, still running on the old "deprecated" GTX 1080 Ti that no one even talks about anymore. Experience degrades with context, even when running a small model, which further degrades my experience.
