# The Boringness That Got Eliminated
essay

Tuck out your screen from your pocket and you can open up social media at the tip of your finger. Oh, how about chatting with other people, the words from various people that filled the front page you don't even have time to check all of them, you wager. Perhaps you want to watch some interesting cat watching Tom and Jerry and put itself in Tom's shoes, jumping onto Jerry and broke the TV screen! Or maybe you just want to read Chinese novels. A screen in your pocket make it all easy -- you sit down, and within a matter of seconds, your head is down. Pedestrian walking past, cars wheezing away, or the rustling winds -- you noticed none, all while your attention were sucked into a limitless black hole. 

Half a century ago, all these were not available. Night time, before the PC were even affordable, people might watch TV or newspaper or something. But TV and radio and news, despite addicting, you had to switch channel to find something interesting, lest one channel don't always show what you want to watch/listen to. You had limited control over what contents you can digest; for changing channels and directly picking a specific documentary at this time is totally different choices made. Then, not everyone can afford to watch TV at all times. There are people who had not had the time to enjoy such luxury -- and they went to work. Or just because they find it not as interesting after the initial fad; their time would not be filled with the limited fantasy it can provide. They went back into their lives to find something interesting to do. 

Not that these people aren't available nowadays. We still can see people in their 20s pursuing startups, or just being a workaholic. Generally, with globalization, the countries which reached the top of their current cycle before dropping into decadence, as proposed by Ray Dalio's Big-Debt-Cycle, were not required to work as hard as their parents and grandparents used to be. 2 generations of hard work had brought sufficient luxury for them to enjoy life more than the previous generations had. The few that got stuck climbing the corporate ladder rung had strong influences from their parents; and those that startups found it more interesting than sticking their face onto the screen. But nevertheless, statistically speaking, the majority of the ability is concentrated in the minority of people -- and the 'ability' includes the ability **to resist** sticking one's face onto the screen -- and the gap is getting larger, the minority smaller, the concentration denser. 

Without the screen to disturb them, and living at a poor family, the previous generations still in their early stage of building wealth had to work to not die of hunger. Pursuing outsiders and strangers opened up more opportunity, not just of work, but also, say, social. These opportunities feed into one's pleasure-seeking centers in the brain, and one forgot about the dopamine released looking at screen alone. What's more interesting than working together towards some goal with people beside you, hand in hand? The social is the important factor. Just look at how much 'convenience' we've created today, and one already felt disgusted. How, one possibly ask, could you detached a person so much they had no need for social interactions with strangers? Even calling the pizza man to order pizza the tension you can feel inside your hard strained, what says randomly speaking to someone sharing a table with you at the restaurant? 

The opportunity is many then, fewer now, not because of monopolies like the Mag 7 companies (Apple, Meta, Google, etc.) but because we, having been influenced by our screen-seeking tendency, tend not to try create more opportunity for many. We, it's we whom had doomed the current generation, we whom had put us in our situation; or do we have to blame the screen being too addictive, that the previous generation had made our life easier but more decadent? Or we whom naïvely made it a fad and chose to stay in it than to face the difficulties of real life tragedies and problems? It's the _struggle_ of problems that made us grow, but one day, we had stopped struggling. No, when we encounter problems nowadays, our first instinct is no longer to fight, but to escape. We rather had no hassle in our lives than trying to get better out of it. A fight between two friends that necessarily emerges a synergized solution in the past might now be a breakup between two friends of long. Plus, it's all too easy to ghost people nowadays that you don't see them face to face -- you can't just turn away and pretend not to know them when they're catching after you, you know. In one's case, while my parents felt no guilty to phone someone anytime in the day regardless of how the person on the other side feels, one had to first text the other side to set a time so it doesn't "disturb them from their busy work", even though, if people phone one, one would probably just hang up and text them back that one'll call them back if one's busy but one won't feel angry at them, how stupidly one assumed they would feel angry at one for doing the same thing. 

The essay starts with one reading a book called Intimate History of Humanity by Theodore Zeldin, a chapter on someone whom pursued a startup because she met him, he opened up a company, and she joined, and break up because they're out of ideas 7 years later (if one remembered correctly the number). And now, one, older than her, is still here at home, not yet pursuing any startups, though of oneself more stupid than her, complaining and rantling that one can never succeed because the world is now different and one no longer believes one can succeed from external influences -- whoever always breaking one's hope by saying to one all day, "you will never succeed. You're not as clever as who who who." The latter sentence is a reply if one rebut with "He/she also successful, how can one not?" Yes, yes, one know one's more stupid than the world, don't even know why one's born into this world if one's as stupid as you say so. The child at the other home are always more clever than the child at your own home, yes. 

One assumed (wrongly) that people in the past without phones are boring. Because they felt boring, they want to pursue more interesting things than wasting their flesh and blood, so they went out and startups. This note had been put here since last few days to rot while one had something else to busied with; and when one come back to it now, one remembered TV. Even in the past past, based on Fernand Braudel's discussion, people in French and whatnots were playing card games and speaking when they had nothing to do. They don't really pursue these more interesting works, prefer to rest whole year long and only be busy for a few months a year than to work whole year long. The argument that one raised, that people whom felt boring and therefore pursue work, is therefore wrong. Since the Industrial Revolution, entrepreneurals had rethink clocks to make 'slaves' work harder to earn them more money, for the same amount of meager pay. And that's when, with luxury rethought, people went out to earn these more money to pursue the luxury life that they'd seen featured in the news, continually whipping them to success, or the success that the minority had framed as a motivation for the lowers to earn more money for them. Perhaps we are no different. 

Can you imagine a distorted Web3 blockchain? People want to pay people for the data they shared in social media? No, one's not against the war against the monopolies of data, that they misuse our personal data and selling it to advertisement and to do other stuffs that we don't give them consent to. Yet, each of us kinda tolerate them because we know that, if we don't want them to use it for whatever, we would not have chosen to use their service in the first place. Just like in public, you had to accept that people will get into your circle (armlength physical distance) because it's the public, no matter how queasy you feel, you had no choice but to accept; it's the same with if you want to use the service of someone else, you had to accept that, one day, your data will be misused. 

The war with social media is never on privacy and personal data -- it's on **time!** The moment you start paying people for sharing data on social media, and if they found out that you pay so much that they can live off it without having to work, and if everyone started doing that, the society is doomed. Though one don't think the society will be doomed that way, for not everyone will be fancied by sitting in front of social media whole day long and will still prefer to work for some purposes, one do think that open the door to such possibility is dangerous -- it means **promoting uselessness**: you're being paid for rotting your own body. It's time to think how to make social media less addictive (against profit) than to spread the net and catch up more and more attention that still had not been netted inside the social media net. 

The boringness had not yet been eliminated; we chose to feel boring.
