# How People Rate An App Is Interesting
ratings apps curious interesting

There's something really interesting. There was an extension from VS Code that one wanted to install just yesterday, suggested by AI, but then one immediately saw that it only have 2.5 stars, for around 750 reviews. Immediately, one dismissed it, trying to find another extension by looking at the list, just to realize that these 4-5 stars app only have less than 10 reviews, or at most a dozens. 

Then, only did one start to check how many _downloads_ the apps received. Surprisingly, those 4-5 stars, despite being 4-5 stars, only receive thousands of downloads, some tens of thousands, but very little reaches hundreds of thousands. And that 2.5 stars app? Nearly 7 million downloads! 

It just means that **people who're satisfied with the app never post about it, and the ones that put a star on it are those that are unsatisfied.** It also proves that _Thunder Client did not hire a group of "watery soldiers," direct translated from its Chinese equivalent, to push its stars up._ Perhaps it just don't find a need for it -- it's productocracy. 

And after one downloaded and used it, it actually fulfilled my expectations. It's more like CURL without the need to type commands in the shell every time (P.S. One don't like it when one have to change things in my shell and if it's very long -- not a geek with shell commands unless it's quick enough -- and no, one hate Vim, VS Code is much better), and it remembers what body one need to put into it, plus one can easily change between GET and PUT and POST in two clicks (via the dropdown). All in all, easy to use. 

That's when one realize -- **always check the no. of downloads compared to the no. of people that comments.** Who would have known that it's deceiving if you didn't check twice?
