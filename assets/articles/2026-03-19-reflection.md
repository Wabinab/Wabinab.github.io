# Update on Life 2026 03
update reflection MJDeMarco

Even before my previous article, one had already felt depressed and felt a need to get out of my house that had chained me for years. Years of my life was gone, just because one feared that if one did not finish paying my debt, one do not deserve to have holidays. But my depression got so bad one finally had to take the steps to mitigate it -- and that means, going out to find new friends. 

One did not regret one's trip, even felt grateful that one did go. One's writing it in a book right now, and will be linked in the About page when it's published months or years from now. One originally framed it as a "2nd inflection point of my life," and indeed, it was and is my 2nd inflection point in life. It's when one got happier, understood that one don't need to rely on Chinese novels and/or gaming to survive the boring days, when one put down everything and just walk alongside someone, feeling dependent on them. It's giving myself up, forgetting my own ego, so that one can feel as one with them. 

And sometimes, one mistaken those dependence as a love for them. Or perhaps it _is_ love. In _The Second Mountain_, David Brooks said that marriage is a willingness to be dependent on someone else, something on that line. One read that book _after_ one came back from the trip, concluding that one should _not_ be dependent on someone else because someone one grew close with told me she did not want to be dependent on someone else, and one tried to mimic her. But if we lack attachment to someone, are we not avoidant? 

Anyways, after coming back, one did not quickly realize the matter with money. 2 months of 'holidays,' more than $6000 SGD spent. That's how much one would earn from about 2 years of savings! It also means that one cannot go out every year lest one produce less than one spend. It means that, though one loves to go out with people once again and hoped that one would have the space and time to do so, without working my ass off for 2 years, one would not be able to pay for my next trip. 

And one settled that to be the truth instead of questioning it. 

At least, not until one saw _that book_ in a Popular bookstore in Malaysia. 

Despite traveling to meet friends, one was still living as frugal as one can without deteriorating my health, without going to the extremes of camping out and be around the homeless, and hopefully, to not eat out as often as one wished to even though it's unhealthy to do so compared to the vegetable soup one's making for dinner almost every night with the same old ingredient that one never gets disgusted about. The problem lies with the choice one have -- feeling chained to _not_ do something not because one cannot but because one cannot _afford_ so. There's a time when one want to visit the labyrinth under the Buda side of Budapest, but an hour tour cost around 10,000 ft (which was around 40 euros) -- although one can afford that, if one don't want to go bankrupt by the end of my trip, one had to frugally halt my own action. And one really don't like that, but one don't know that one don't like that until one read _that book_. 

_Unscripted_, by MJ Demarco. 

One started out disgusted by his first sentence introduction, not because it wasn't interesting, but because there's an excessive use of foul words and "broken English" (i.e. sentences that should break with a comma was instead broken with a full stop, and sentences that should form a paragraph together were instead broken into several paragraph, each with only that single sentence, his writing made me feel like he was some self-help gurus and one had grown tired of these self-help gurus). One quickly put the book down before even reading the 2nd sentence, only to come back later after one'd browse through the other books that one'd recorded down from the same store and found that they couldn't light up my urge to read.

One had discovered that one was in the 'trough' of reading, the period where one felt one had to read to learn more, but one had no heart for reading anything. 

And it was MJ's book that rekindled my spirit to read again. In fact, his book would not have rekindled my heart if one had read it back in 2018, when one was struggling to make and keep friends, when one was struggling to keep oneself out of one's depression, one would have thrown the book away and say, "Go away! You're not even teaching me how to make friends, how to keep friends close to my side, you're useless to me!" 

In his book, he mentioned the turning point called "Fuck-this event (FTE)", and that was when one was still early of my FTE in the realm of friendship, not economics. 

But now, after years of getting stuck in a job that pay little money (though quite lenient with time, though one don't know what to do with that time except to read more and more books), the years of struggle one had with my debt, and the requirement of money to forward oneself in one's own selfish life goals, one could no longer take it. 

In 2024, one thought stock market was the way. No, not the safe play with ETF, but gambling and hope that a single stock would go up up up up up and up, straight to the sky (like how some shitcoins in crypto goes from $0.5 'per coin' to $500 'per coin' overnight, that kind of straight up to the sky), cash out, and violà! One was taking one's advice from the investor-turned-author Nassim Nicholas Taleb that if you spread out and buy small and buy enough of them, eventually, occurrences will take you to the black swan. And do that many times and you get rich in a year and a half. 

But unfortunately, one wish that one can see the future. One had not been granted with a forward vision that could push my way to riches. And now one needed something solid. 

So that's what one's pursuing right now, the teachings of MJ. One don't know if one'll succeed or not in the future. There are a few ideas in my mind, one's pursuing one of them that feels like 0% Excellent execution (because it depends on some stupid kids feeling so interested about it and steal money from their parents to buy it) though maybe 1.5% or 2% for average execution (useful to some industries, if they find it useful, though one don't know). Maybe that can work, but if one find a better idea that has at least a 0.01% on Excellent education in the future, one might change to that. For now, just execute, and see where it'll take us. Who knows, it might teach me something that's lacking in this field and one would change to it, like that guy in MJ's book that wanted to publish a book but found that there's no one making various covers for the same book to be distributed among the readers and see which one works best? Perhaps life may come in that way. 

The only problem with pursuing brick-and-mortar is capital upfront, and unless one lend from the bank (which isn't clever since one's still paying back my school debt), one cannot be sure that one can do things in that field. So one told oneself, maybe in the future. Unless one can find something that don't need that much capital to get started. One can't afford to fail if otherwise.

That's about where my life is right now. In the future, one'll probably update more again on how this is going. And hopefully, _our_ future will be brighter and happier together.
