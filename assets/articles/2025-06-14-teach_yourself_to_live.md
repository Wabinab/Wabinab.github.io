# Teach Yourself To Live By C.G.L. Du Cann
afterthought self_help self_improvement

If there's one book that changed how one think about the world, more grounded in reality, it's this book. Teach Yourself to Live is not a self-help book like any others that uses brainwashing to achieve its effect. The latter will tell you, live is hard, but there's something you can learn from me to solve such problem. It's _exactly_ the same techniques employed by brainwashers when they try to manipulate people. But Teach Yourself to Live? You're beaten to the ground, recognizing how you are just one of the many, nothing too special, because everyone is special, and the supply of "specialness" hence exceeded the demand for it. 

Mr. Fukuoka once said, in a book that someone compiled his teaching, that goes on the line of recognizing you're _completely powerless_ against Nature, that trying to manipulate Nature ultimately backfires, like a dam that keeps trying to push back water but ultimately cannot but collapse when just a crack happens. Constructing artificial dams to power electricity was a stupid idea to begin with, though one can't explain why beavers build dam, but one hypothesizes that it had to do with retaining the water when the seasons goes dry, or when the land needs enough water to rejuvenate its damaged Nature. More research is required on this topic. Du Cann started his book by making us realizes that "**desperate facts unwilled by you, and which you are _completely powerless_ to alter**" speaks right to the heart. We try, try so hard, to take things in control, only to realize that they're never things we can control, and does nothing except to salve your mind by lying to yourself. He tries to make us realize that "You are You, not a better or worse Anybody Else." In essence, your parents might always be comparing you to some other "children role models", deprecating you, but... they're not you, unfortunately. In fact, that's kinda stupid. My parents used to say, oh my God, why don't we have girls in our children? Look at the neighbors, their girls would do everything for them, how comfortable they felt; and look at us, our boys just sit lazily in their room doing their own stuffs, refusing to help out. What a menace. Of course, people speaks in black and white terms, and that's rather exaggerated, but you know the line. Except that, what they never see, is how the neighbor girls were raised up that makes them take initiatives. One was never raised up to take initiative, as far as one remember, only to be protected under their wings? And one doubt, if they have a girl, the girl would be helping out than be more like the boys they now have -- me and my sibling(s) -- sitting inside one's room and doing one's things, because ultimately, it's how they raised us up that matters. Girls don't magically born to be hardworking. They learn by imitation, and probably, by social expectations. 

Oh, how we wish to have supernatural powers, to be the special one. One is just the same, if you think so too. But alas, Du Cann said, if we can't come out of it, we'll be befogged and bewildered forever by such false and misleading illusions about oneself and one's life, which _most people_ do. But even if we aren't supernatural beings or have superpowers, it's crucial to take care of our body and mind. "A Driver and Blade are one in body and soul," said the folks in Xenoblade 2. "And this self is a body and a mind, not merely one or the other," said Du Cann. 

But why do we live? "**As long as you live, you count. When you're dead, you don't.**" is the answer Du Cann give. Most people aren't exceptional beings in the exceptions. We're just normal people doing normal stuffs going through life that we can't choose normal or not. It's not your choice when you see bombs dropped over your head; and if you're not an immigrant, it might not even be your choice to live there in the first place! Like money, **time** is of the essence, even when one have to admit guilty to it, for frittering a large portion away to reading super-addictive Chinese novels, and a smaller portion of them (in aggregate, cumulative since one was born) on watching cartoons/animations, and playing games, as the 3 main fruitless attempts one indulged and still indulges in. But life is short -- slightly less than 5000 weeks, given our current average lifespan. With one writing now, it's another Saturday, and one week had just passed, motes evaporating away from one without one realizing -- or, more accurately, without one wanting to realize. "On the Shortness of Life" by Seneca still rings true. 

And we don't even have choice for some of the things. Life is born with terms and conditions, not a gift upon us for free. Whether we agree with them or not, one must abide by them; and abiding by them means the more advantageous. Such are another teaching of Du Cann. There are other teachings, but one won't list them all here, though some includes: 

- Don't copy others or conform unnecessarily to their conventions or opinions. 
- There are limits which all our efforts cannot transcend.
