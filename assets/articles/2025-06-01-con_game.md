# The Confidence Game By Maria Konnikova
afterthought stranger

Actually, this isn't an afterthought, but one's trying to sort out one's mind, so one'll write it out. 

One was trying to translate the word "con artist" to Chinese. It translates to "诈骗者" or "欺诈者", but one thought that's not correct. If you translate the Chinese words to English, you get "cheaters". Ultimately, the words suggests the _result_, but what we want to know, is the _why_, and that's where the English word "con artist" explains more about. 

诈骗 and 欺诈 means getting cheated, but _what exactly_ is that you're being cheated of? You might say money. Sure, that might be true, but what exactly you get cheated? No, it's not your money, even if you lose money. What you get cheated is the word "con", which stands for "*confidence*". Or in other words, _trust_. That isn't reflect in the Chinese word that is in the English's, and that's where one think the definition is lacking. 

There are a gazillion of ways to con someone, and it's always updating as our world evolved. Interestingly, despite our advance in technology and other fields, like getting most people to have minimal education to at least finishing primary school, we're not less likely to be conned. Con appeals to your emotion, not to your logic, and you, as a human and not a robot, will most likely get conned, unless you're emotionless, like a real psychopath (which is very rare) is.[1]

Confidence game could happen on many dimensions, but one really wanted to speak on a single dimension. In that dimension, we have a continuum from downright stupid person, to genius, Einstein! You might assume, the more clever you are, the less you are to fall for a scam, yes? But no, you're wrong. In fact, there are some scams that increase its probability of success the more clever you are, and we'll see why in a second. Let's start with stupid cons. 

One example of stupid cons nowadays is phishing emails. They have some button or links in the email sent to you, and as you click in, redirect to a legitimate-looking website, but it's actually fake. The moment you enter the details, your details are saved in the phisher's database, and they can use it to hack your account. If one is the phisher, one'll also redirect the user to the real page and ask them to enter the details one more time again, so they'll be log into the real site, done their job, and now it's _my turn_. They're specifically target for the stupid because of their legitimacy. When you look at the email, sufficiently clever people will found the English language bad, **utterly bad.** Only the most stupid of them will fall for it and ignore the bad English. But why was it bad? Is it because the phishers don't understand English? Coming from a country like Nigeria that you assume they don't know English? **Wrong!** Even if the phisher is a native English/American, (s)he'll also _purposefully_ write in bad English for his/her scam. 

You see, when Internet first started out, the phishers designed the email to be _too legitimate_ looking, with _too_ professional an English, that it looks even more real than the legitimate one. At the end, almost everyone falls for the con. Now there's a problem -- if the con wants to continue to the next step and steal their info, who would they choose? They can't go for all, because there's **too many** of them. It's difficult to decide who's stupid enough that they can see it through the end. So in the end, they learnt it the hard way -- incorporate a filter at the very beginning to filter out those tech savvy and genius, even moderate people, and let only the most stupid fall for the con. The bad English? It's purposefully done to only attract the most stupid. **For the stupid, you want them to place their confidence in _you_, the con artist.** 

On the other hand, that type of con won't attract the geniuses. But don't worry, we have another type of con that specifically targets the geniuses. The more genius they are, the more likely they fall for the con. The more they know about the subject you're about to con them on, the more likely they'll fall for it. **It's the time when knowledge backfires!** You thought, by knowing more about it, you're less likely to get cheated -- but that's the game. By knowing more about it, you're more likely to _trust_ yourself, you're more likely to be _confident_ about yourself, about what you know. One can then design the story that tries to lure you to be vague enough such that, when there's an empty space in between, you'll use _**your** knowledge_ to _fill in the blanks!_ And that's done in subconscious, so you _don't know_ you had just fill in the blanks yourself, but one know it all right. One'll then, from your line, continue the story, slowly by slowly, taking you to the well without you knowing. **The best thing about this con is, even after you fall for it, because you're so confident you're too clever to fall for a con, you would convince yourself that you didn't fall for a con, so after one successfully, say, steal your money once, one can do it on you again and again and again, until you rip your whole bank off, and you'll still believe _in yourself_ that you didn't fall for a scam.** Actually, it's also the same for the stupid one, if they learn nothing from it and didn't become more clever out of it. Only that, contrast to the stupid one, **you want them to place confidence in _themselves_, NOT in you.** Such con always works, and the more knowledgeable they are, the more confident they are, hence the more likely they'll fall for it. 

Of course, seriously speaking, that may or may not be enough. Only for those people (which is the majority) that **really wanted to save face** will they fall for the con game again and again. What about those people who could laugh at themselves when they did something stupid? Now, you need some other anchor to hold them on. One of the ways you could do that is by taking advantage of their _lose aversion_ mentality. 

When you put in a stake, you naturally want something to come out of it. E.g. when you invest money, you want your money to grow. But we, including myself, are impatient, so we want them to grow fast. People as poor as me, whom could only place, say, $100 in the investment market, cannot grow a lot if the market only promises 10% return per year, _on average_. If one put it for 30 years, one'll only have $1745 in the account! That's too slow, and too little. One wants $100 to become $1,000,000 in, say, 3 years! You see, investment only works if you already have a lot of money; otherwise, you'll have to pass it on as heritage, and you never live to see your investment flourish. That's called 前人种树，后人乘凉。Ancestors plant trees, their descendants get to live under the shade, _not them_. You need something to explode your money up, and the only way you could get that is by gambling. You gamble that something will explode 1000x in a year. Hypothetically, you borrowed the eye of God, and you sell it "just before it drops". Then, you do that again and again, and you'll be rich. Except we don't have the eyes of God, nor do God lend us His/Her eyes, so we need someone whom _have_ the Eyes of God. We truly believe that some people on Earth really were granted by God His/Her eyes, the ability to foresee the future. We want them to help us foresee where will our investment goes, and so we'll buy "on time", sell "on time", ignoring possible delays. Now that's where the game start, and there are 2 ways you could fall for the game. 

First, you made a choice to fall for the con artist game. You go to someone who can see constellations, horoscopes, the message from the stars, or having blessed by God to foresee the future through whatever means (cards, mahjong, etc.), and pay them to foresee for you. From the con artist perspective, they don't need to pick their marks out of the crowd -- you approach them yourself. They know you're **desperate**. Perhaps one of your relative have cancer, and you really need the money to multiply in 3 months to pay the bill. In such case, you're **emotionally hijacked.** When people are emotionally hijacked, they'll do anything, stupid or not. Why do people commit suicide, have the _courage_ to jump off buildings? Most people don't understand -- that's because they try to predict _rationally_, _logically_. But alas, the person up there had lose access to his/her prefrontal cortex, the area of your brain that defines logic. In an emotional hijack, all you could think of is the loud and clear voice, "End it all. Just jump off, and all of this ends. You won't have to suffer anymore. You'll _finally be free!_" Rationally, we thought it won't happen to us. **But beware -- you might be that guy/girl some day!** 

Second, someone saw you're _desperate_, or at least _assume_ you're desperate, from how you act on the market, and approaches you. Or they may not know about you, they may know there will be people on the market that's desperate, so they post out an ad. "Double your money in 3 months", says the ad. You saw the ad, there's something magical swirling in your brain, you keep repeating the words. Your heart pumped faster, your sweat perspire, you told yourself, "One'll just go in and see what it is. One'll certainly not fall for it. It doesn't hurt if one just see, right? One's not even taking an extra action. Perhaps the guy[2] might be legitimate, you know? If he's not, one'll quit. One'll have _control_ over when one'll quit. Let's just check this out, _just for this once_." 

Bullshit. You see how many people tried drugs and never came out of it? Smoke and never came out of it? Alcohol and never came out of it? That's the same situation happening here. **You thought you're in control, but Mr. Fukuoka once said, _nothing_, and one repeat, NOTHING, is in your control.** You have absolutely NO CONTROL over you can come out of it or not. The moment you step into it, you're done for. The moment your desperation, translated into your action to magically click on the link, you're in, _all in_ already. 

That's just a few of the con games someone can play. There are a lot of con games happening, but most appeal to your emotions -- it could be 'positive' emotions like greed and jealousy, or it could be 'negative' emotion like despair, desperation, and desolation. It doesn't matter how clever you are -- in fact, some con artists love clever people, because they're more likely to fall for their con than people with only average knowledge that want the con artist to fill in the blanks for them instead of them filling in the blanks themselves. As long as humans have an emotion call "confidence", in oneself (a.k.a. self-confidence) or in others (a.k.a. trust), the con game will never ends, no matter how tech savvy our world had become, no matter how educated you are. While you may be normal and won't fall for a scam in most of the days, 99.9% of the days; there's that 0.1% that you'll be sadder than usual, or more greedy than usual. That's when the con artist took advantage and had you go _all in_ before you call it quit. That's when **you had nowhere to go except to fall for it.** In fact, you have _no choice but to fall for it._ It'd been designed specifically to let _you_ fall for it, just like the personalized ads large companies showed us to "improve your shopping experiences". And you just fall for it.
