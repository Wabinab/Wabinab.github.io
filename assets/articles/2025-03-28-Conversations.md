# Conversations By Theodore Zeldin
strangers afterthought

Conversation isn't talking. Talking does not change people; conversation does, however slightly. You start with a willingness to emerge as a slightly different person, for the positive or for the negative, by changing the way you see the world, even participating in changing the world. But that's difficult -- for nowadays, everyone wants to speak, fighting for the chance to open their mouth, whether or not anyone is listening. This isn't the first time we wished for conversations; several encounters in history only convince a _minority_: most preferred their way of speaking despite. Plus, conversation is a two-way thing. It's easy if both person agrees to the 'rules' of conversation, an exchange of speaking and listening roles; it's not easy if you're the one trying to converse but the other person just throws whatever at you, refusing to listen. Asking them to listen doesn't work: they'll have lots of smiling excuses that the smiles encourages you to not reject their excuses. Plus, one person listening to you conveying info or emotions isn't conversation. A one-way communication isn't a conversation. 

What we talk about also matters. Some sharpen the mind, some contains no nourishment. The ones that sharpen the mind most are those with a different mind than yours. No, they're not the one that shares a favorite passion like football with you. And of course, they're not the one that cheers for a particular team, be it Red Sox, or Liverpool, or whatever, like you. Indeed, you might like football but they like basketball. Or you might be artistic and they be scientific. A topic of focus posed, the artistic you see it from a different angle than the scientific them, and both of you discussing brings each other more towards from the extreme to the center. You might not be convinced by their explanations, and that's OK, yet, please don't raise barriers to shield your view, deeming yours superior to them when it's not. In author's words, conversation don't just reshuffle the cards (of choices you can make); it **create new cards**. We can make our own choice, a choice which isn't already listed on the table. 

You don't need to be a somebody to converse with others. Ordinary people are those whom come up with extraordinary statements. That's reasonable, because elites are only a few people, a small percentage to the ordinary. Even if elites have a higher probability of coming up with extraordinary sentence, the sheer number of the mundane, you'd expect the latter to come up with more no. of extraordinary sentences. Though, to speak up, you need **courage.** You might not think of yourself as brave, but courage lets you do brave things otherwise. 

Between men and women, Guazzo proposed a love language: **civil conversation.** That's living together in decency, without quarrels or violence. Plus, he introduced certain actions like honesty and kindness, attentiveness to woman's feelings, etc. They're useful, except people continue to admire violence, even after so long. In contrast, the romantic language picked up later, popularized by poets and novelists in its perfect ideal to the mundane. Perfect ideal executed by imperfect human means impossible to attain. Indeed, 2 things are unacceptable: it mentioned you don't need to know your partner properly, and it mentioned you should not control, even if you have control, over who and how and when to fall in love, and let the seed germinate then die off quickly, and should not put effort into maintenance of the relationship. 

Many conversations still dies quickly, like fire without fuel. Sure, stereotypes is one of the reason that prevented classes from mixing, be it men and women, or skin color, or employer and employee, or rich and poor, and old and young, etc. The lack of curiosity is another thing: to find other people of other classes neither interesting nor intelligible. Intelligible because one is not willing to put in the effort to make it legible. Uninteresting because one is the center at which the sun and the Earth rotates around. But there's a limit to what one can know about oneself, hence a limit on what one can talk about oneself. There can be no satisfactory conversation without mutual **respect**. But how're people supposed to know what to talk about in a good relationship? 

We need role models. But it's hard to show how relationships go right, how conversations are good, than the bright red bleeping light when they goes wrong, noticed by everyone unless so ignorant. To destroy class difference means to treat everyone as equals. Now that men and women are equally educated and doing the same job, the inequality had been smaller and smaller. For courage, it's difficult to only grow self-confidence without being arrogant. How we speak: **they don't come naturally**. We need to put more thought into it. If you argue, you might win your arguments, but both of you would end up in mutual exhaustion, compared to the continual refreshments most get out of conversations. 

And most doesn't mean everyone. Some just cannot get it, though. They see conversations of any sorts as a monster lurking out there that makes them lethargy after more than a fixed period of time, say, 30 minutes. By the by, yawning is **NOT** a good indicator of them feeling tired -- one always do that, yawning, during conversations, yet one's brain is clearer than ever. On the other hand, when one feels bored by your conversation, one'll look left and right, look at the clock, all stuffs to signal that the other person to stop; but of course, those that makes one boring are usually those arrogant people whom don't want to stop despite all little messages you conversed to them, speak on nonetheless, until you directly confront them to cut the crap, then you'll start a short argument with many buts and whatsoever that they want to finish what they're speaking regardless of how you feel before they're willing to go away, which will take another, what, 15-30 minutes? Maybe shorter, if they're even slightly compassionate. 

Have you ever felt your parents treating you like a kid, even when you're not a kid anymore? One does, though not everyone did. One surely doesn't feel like speaking to one's parents, categorizing them as "unable to get one's message across" due to the lack of respect, the seeing that one is forever lower than them, hence one's opinions aren't worth listening to. At least that's how one feels and get the gist of. The range of conversation is strictly limited. My younger brother, one don't know what he feels, but he can speak to my dad a lot, cause they share similar viewpoints, share similar passions -- but we already told you that the most brilliant mixing comes from different viewpoints, different passions -- hence they could talk and enjoy cars, work/jobs, etc. that one had absolutely no interest in, not as strong as relationship, which my parents deemed as not useful -- they're time wasters, they said, that took precious time to do other things like washing cars, cutting grass and tree branches, etc. away from them. 

Yet we need the family. For your siblings at long range, they are really strangers; and you practice with them, via the bond of blood that brought you together, what you are to speaking to strangers. Such gardens can expand to the world of gardens therefore, to speak with those where you're not bound by blood, however thin, in various circumstances, like over a meal. No, don't keep looking at the food in front of you; share what you had, how you feel about them, with the one sitting next or in front of you. Food is an opener to deeper conversations, just like weather is a small talk that leads to deeper conversations. Food incites a topic on cooking, preparing of meals. What quality of conversations you can have during a meal than just the décor of the restaurants or the technicalities of the récipés. 

The limited topic of work, particularly, of specialist, makes it hard to get new insights across boundaries. It's not just going in and see many different people walking in and out that helps -- if they don't converse, nothing changes. Nowadays, tired with jobs, we need jobs that suit people, not people that suit jobs. Is it unrealistic? No, not really. The people during the Renaissance did it before, by becoming a generalist. Today's manager are somewhat a generalist, but they're amateurs at it: nowhere they can be trained to become one. Indeed, business school trained them to be more profit-minded. To start with, we need to change the quality of conversation at work. Get rid of some barriers we raised from sharing our thoughts with 'outsiders'. Don't separate work with life: they should be one and for all, enjoyed as leisure. The only reason we separate them is because we don't like our job. And that's why we need to create new job. 

The more we see of the world, the broader is the range of our curiosity. 

What technology do to the ability to converse, we'd already see. Unfortunately, it'd not grow as author wished it to be -- for emails as a tool to reach out to strangers and make more pen-pal. Instead they'd been abused by companies to send out mails between already-known only, and abused by hackers to send malicious mails to you, and abused by other companies to send various newsletters to you; all the while flood your email with unlimited stuffs when your institution (say, your university) sell your email address, created by them, to third party who send you advertising mails. Surely, the creation of train supposingly allow strangers to meet each other, but only in the economy class where many aggregate. The first class and business class are for those secluded in their giant luxurious prison. But that's for the past; nowadays, the economy class of planes had been colluded by screens installed at the back of every seat for the people sitting behind you to watch whatever entertainment they could, even playing games! The ability to speak to everyone was robbed from us once more. For trains, one had only traveled in UK's trains: they're some seated in 4s, but if it's not full and you brought with you luggages, people would prefer not to seat beside you. Even if they seat in front of you, it's now the norm to not speak with them, and don't even look at them. You can look out of the window or look down on your phone, or, like most of them, immediately go to sleep the moment they board the train, taking time off from the never-stopping schedule they flooded them with outside traveling and 'catching up' on sleep therefore. What's more, some of the seats are back facing, like those on planes; who are you going to talk to? The person seated next to you, they're doing their stuffs, not interested in you, or talking on the phone, or just looking at their phone the moment they get themselves seated. Planes without screens are better in a way that, before they go to sleep, you muster your courage and start speaking with them, even if it's fragmented, at least you took your first step. The idea is not to seat with those you already know and had chance to speak elsewhere, like at home; instead, choose a seat where you're surrounded by strangers, and you maximize your chance to, if the one sitting on your left don't want to speak to you, speak to the one on your right; and if not, the one in front of you, provided they're facing you in a 4s, for example. Tech doesn't automatically improve conversation, communication, or behavior -- you still have to muster the courage and take the effort to take the first step to do so. 

The way we talk about failure: we need courage to face failure, to resist disappointment, and be immune to the cynicism like self-deprecating comments or whatsoever. 

It's OK to be silent while you watch ideas meet. Some people emanates this radiance that encourage others to talk, but they themselves don't. Of course, talk without thought is empty. Watch lonely ideas intermingle themselves and what sparks released. Conversation need pauses, long pauses, for you need the time to think. If the other person keeps talking, it's OK to ask them to pause and wait for you while they have a drink and think for themselves; encourage them not to look at their phone while letting you think for a while. Don't cheat because you're scared of being too deep or too personal. Don't limit our curiosity to a certain direction; broaden it up!

Yes, the watery words one found are the examples, but without the examples, you can't fully see it, so they're not really watery.
